What the questions look like

Examiners test three-dimensional lines through several reliable problem patterns:

  • Shortest Distance Between Skew Lines: Given two skew lines in symmetric form, compute the shortest distance connecting them using the scalar triple product formula or finding endpoints of the common perpendicular.
  • Foot of Perpendicular and Spatial Image: Dropping a perpendicular from a point onto a line, finding the coordinates of the foot by enforcing orthogonality with direction ratios, and determining the reflected point.
  • Direction Cosines from Simultaneous Relations: Solving a linear and a quadratic relation in direction ratios l, m, and n to deduce the angle between two lines.
  • Line Intersecting Two Given Lines: Constructing a third line intersecting two given skew lines at points C and D, followed by evaluating the distance squared between C and D.

How to practise it

Mastering three-dimensional lines requires disciplined parametric thinking:

  • Express points on a line parametrically: Set the symmetric line ratio equal to lambda. A general point on the line then has coordinates linearly dependent on lambda, converting geometric intersections and perpendicularity checks into simple linear equations.
  • Master the shortest distance formula: For lines r equals a1 plus lambda b1 and r equals a2 plus mu b2, the shortest distance is the magnitude of the dot product of a2 minus a1 with the cross product of b1 and b2, divided by the magnitude of that cross product.
  • Enforce orthogonality via dot products: To locate the foot of the perpendicular from an external point to a line, represent the foot as a general parametric point F(lambda). The vector connecting the external point to F must have a zero dot product with the direction vector of the line.
  • Recognise coplanar lines: Two lines are coplanar if they either intersect or are parallel. Setting the determinant formed by the difference vector a2 minus a1 and both direction vectors equal to zero directly provides the condition for coplanarity.

Frequently asked questions

Are plane equations completely excluded from JEE Main now?

Yes, plane equations were explicitly removed from the official NTA syllabus starting with the 2024 examination. Recent papers contain zero questions on planes, focusing entirely on spatial lines, direction cosines, and vector projections.

How do I check if two lines in space intersect?

Compute the shortest distance between them. If the scalar triple product of the vector joining a point on each line with the two direction vectors equals zero, the lines lie in a common plane. If their direction vectors are not parallel, they intersect at a single unique point.
