What the questions look like

Organ pipe questions evaluate natural acoustic modes. A classic problem states that the difference between the sixth and third harmonics of an open organ pipe equals 2200 Hz and asks for the physical length of the pipe. Students use the open-pipe harmonic formula f_n = n v / (2L), setting the frequency gap to 3 v / (2L) and solving for length using the speed of sound. Another frequent variant matches the fifth harmonic of a closed pipe with the fundamental frequency of an open pipe, asking for their length ratio.

String wave problems test transverse propagation speed v = √(T/μ). A typical question joins two strings of different linear mass densities end-to-end under a shared 500 N tension, asking for the ratio of transit times for pulses initiated at opposite boundaries. Sound velocity questions test gas temperature dependencies, asking for the absolute temperature where sound speed in air doubles relative to its value at 0 °C.

Superposition questions probe beats and interference. A mobile acoustic receiver moves past two coherent speakers separated by a fixed baseline, recording intensity maxima as path differences shift by integer wavelengths. Beats problems evaluate the frequency change when tuning fork prongs are loaded with wax or filed down.

An exceptionally high fraction comes as numerical entry: 50 of the 125 questions since 2019 had no answer options, demanding rigorous unit conversion and arithmetic.

How to practise it

Master boundary conditions for standing waves. Remember that a displacement node occurs at a rigid string clamp or closed organ pipe end (where longitudinal air motion is prevented), whereas an antinode forms at a free string boundary or open organ pipe end (where acoustic pressure matches atmospheric pressure). Open pipes support all integer harmonics (1, 2, 3, ...), whereas closed pipes support only odd harmonics (1, 3, 5, ...).

In string mechanics, memorize that pulse speed depends on tension T and linear mass density μ (mass per unit length). If a string hangs under its own weight, tension varies along its length, so speed increases from bottom to top and calculating transit time requires direct integration.

For sound velocity in gases, apply the Laplace-corrected formula v = √(γRT / M). Notice that sound speed is proportional to the square root of absolute temperature T in Kelvin, but is completely independent of pressure changes at constant temperature because gas density scales proportionally with pressure.

Frequently asked questions

Is Waves regularly tested in JEE Main 2027?

Yes. Waves produced 125 questions across 162 papers in our collection from 2019 to 2026, averaging 0.8 questions per paper. It ranks 22nd of 29 Physics chapters and appeared in 104 of the 162 papers in our set.

Why do closed organ pipes produce only odd harmonics?

A closed pipe requires a displacement node at the sealed bottom and an antinode at the open top. This boundary constraint demands an odd number of quarter wavelengths inside the tube: L = (2n - 1)λ / 4. Consequently, only odd integer multiples of the fundamental frequency can form resonant standing waves.

How does air temperature affect acoustic resonance frequency?

Because the speed of sound scales with the square root of absolute temperature, warmer air increases sound velocity. Since pipe length remains virtually constant, all resonant harmonic frequencies shift upward proportionally with the square root of absolute temperature.
