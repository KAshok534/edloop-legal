Most-asked topics

The syllabus divides across three core topics:

  • Quantitative Analysis: The largest topic with 74 total questions (67 in 2019 to 2026), examined continuously through 2026. This area centers on elemental estimations: Dumas and Kjeldahl methods for nitrogen, Carius method for halogens and sulfur, and precipitation as magnesium pyrophosphate for phosphorus.
  • Purification Methods: Yielded 61 total questions (57 in 2019 to 2026), tested through 2026. Focuses on distillation techniques (steam distillation for aniline, vacuum distillation for glycerol decomposition prevention), paper and thin layer chromatography (Rf values), and column chromatography.
  • Qualitative Analysis: Contributed 26 total questions (20 in 2019 to 2026), evaluated through 2025. Covers sodium fusion extract preparation (Lassaigne's test), detection of nitrogen via Prussian blue, sulfur via sodium nitroprusside (purple coloration), and halogens via silver nitrate precipitates.

What the questions look like

Examiners heavily favor direct arithmetic calculations for elemental percentages. A classic numerical question gives sample mass, volume and normality of standardized acid consumed by evolved ammonia, and asks for the nitrogen percentage via the Kjeldahl formula.

In purification, questions describe physical property differences between mixture components (such as boiling point gaps or steam volatility) and ask candidates to choose the correct separation procedure. Lassaigne extract questions test why sodium metal is used and which nitrogenous compounds (like hydrazine or diazonium salts) fail the test.

How to practise it

Mastering this chapter requires memorizing elemental formulas alongside purification criteria:

1. Memorize quantitative estimation formulas: Be fluent with percentage equations for Carius halogens, Carius sulfur, Kjeldahl nitrogen, Dumas nitrogen (using dry nitrogen volume at STP), and phosphorus. 2. Remember exceptions to Kjeldahl's method: Nitrogen in nitro groups, azo groups, and pyridine rings does not convert into ammonium sulfate under Kjeldahl conditions. 3. Classify distillation techniques: Know exact classic examples: aniline purifies by steam distillation; glycerol purifies by distillation under reduced pressure; petroleum fractions separate by fractional distillation. 4. Learn Lassaigne test colors: Prussian blue for nitrogen, violet for sulfur with nitroprusside, and yellow precipitate soluble in excess ammonia for phosphate. 5. Practise rapid arithmetic: With 49 numerical questions recorded between 2019 and 2026, calculation speed without calculator aid is critical.

Frequently asked questions

Why does hydrazine give a negative Lassaigne test for nitrogen?

Hydrazine contains nitrogen but lacks carbon, so it cannot form sodium cyanide upon sodium fusion, leading to a negative Prussian blue test.

What is the principle behind steam distillation?

Immature boiling occurs when the sum of vapor pressures of water and the immiscible organic liquid equals atmospheric pressure, allowing high boiling heat-sensitive compounds like aniline to distill below their normal boiling point.

Are numerical questions common in this chapter?

Yes, elemental estimation problems are among the most frequent numerical questions in chemistry, with 49 numerical questions set between 2019 and 2026. Step-by-step solutions for each one are in the EdLoop app.
