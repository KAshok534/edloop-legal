How often it comes up

The s-block elements covers alkali and alkaline earth metals and was formerly in the Class 11 curriculum before being removed in 2024, ranking 20 of 31 chapters with a 2.3% share of the chemistry section. During the 2019 to 2026 exam window, s-Block Elements accounted for 104 questions across 162 test papers, taking a 2.3% share of the chemistry section and ranking 20 of 31 chapters. Prior to 2019, from 2002 to 2018, it registered 26 questions across 38 papers with a 1.9% share. In the recent 2024 to 2026 seasons, the chapter saw 0 questions across 58 papers, delivering a 0.0% share. The overall trajectory has trended down following curriculum reduction.

Because the topic was formally deleted, it was not asked in 2024, 2025, or 2026. Prior to removal, the chapter averaged 0.6 questions per paper between 2019 and 2026, including 5 numerical answer questions.

Most-asked topics

Historical questions were split nearly evenly between two groups:

  • Alkaline Earth Metals: Composed 68 total questions (52 during 2019 to 2026), last tested in 2023. Key topics involved hydration enthalpies, sulfate and carbonate thermal stabilities, solubility trends of hydroxides versus sulfates, anomalous covalent chemistry of beryllium, and industrial manufacturing of cement and quicklime.
  • Alkali Metals: Accounted for 62 total questions (52 during 2019 to 2026), last examined in 2023. Primary themes centered on standard reduction potentials, liquid ammonia solutions producing deep blue solvated electrons, oxide and superoxide formation (such as KO₂ paramagnetism), the Solvay ammonia-soda process, and thermal decomposition of nitrates.

What the questions look like

Exam items were heavily factual, checking specific chemical reactions and comparative physical trends. Standard patterns included ordering hydration enthalpies across monovalent and divalent cations, explaining why lithium carbonate decomposes on heating while other alkali metal carbonates remain stable, and identifying magnetic behaviors of normal oxides, peroxides, and superoxides.

Questions on industrial compounds evaluated raw materials and intermediate species formed during the Solvay process or reactions involved in setting plaster of Paris.

Questions to try first

Candidates consulting past question papers for broader competitive chemistry preparation can review these selections:

  • Industrial compound applications and biology: Connecting everyday industrial and biological roles to common calcium and sodium compounds.
  • Alkali metal reduction potentials and hydrides: Assessing multi-statement assertions regarding alkali metal electrode potentials and hydride properties.
  • Solvay process chemical intermediates: Tracing chemical formulas of reagents and by-products in the industrial synthesis of sodium carbonate.
  • Lithium nitrate thermal decomposition: Determining the specific oxide and gaseous products released upon calcining lithium nitrate.
  • Oxide magnetic characteristics: Distinguishing diamagnetic normal oxides from paramagnetic superoxides across alkali metals.

How to practise it

Because this unit has been dropped from the active syllabus, streamline your focus appropriately:

1. Prioritize active chapters: Direct inorganic chemistry study hours into Coordination Compounds and p-Block Elements rather than obsolete units. 2. Maintain awareness of flame test colors: Characteristic flame emissions for sodium, potassium, and calcium occasionally intersect with qualitative salt analysis. 3. Review solubility rules: Grasping why alkaline earth metal sulfate solubility decreases down the group provides helpful chemical intuition.

Frequently asked questions

Is s-Block Elements part of the current JEE Main exam?

No, the National Testing Agency deleted the s-block chapter in the 2024 syllabus rationalization, with 0 questions asked across the 2024, 2025, and 2026 tests.

Do I need to know sodium and potassium compounds for other topics?

Basic chemical formulas like sodium hydroxide or sodium chloride appear as reagents across organic and physical chemistry, but isolated industrial manufacturing details are not examined.
