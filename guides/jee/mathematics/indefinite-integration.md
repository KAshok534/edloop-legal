## Most-asked topics

Antiderivative problems in JEE Main cluster across five primary methods:

1. **Standard Integrals & Substitution (39 questions, 2019-2026; 51 total, last asked 2026)**
The largest category covers clever algebraic substitutions, converting trigonometric expressions into powers of tangent or cotangent, and factoring out high powers of x from radical denominators to reveal standard derivative structures.

2. **Integration by Parts (16 questions, 2019-2026; 20 total, last asked 2026)**
Focuses on product forms combining polynomials with trigonometric or exponential terms. A recurring pattern involves the exponential product form where the integrand splits into the sum of a function and its derivative multiplied by an exponential factor.

3. **Trigonometric Integrals (8 questions, 2019-2026; 20 total, last asked 2026)**
Involves manipulating powers and products of sines, cosines, tangents, and secants using half-angle formulas, symmetric identities, and auxiliary angle methods.

4. **Special Techniques (7 questions, 2019-2026; 10 total, last asked 2026)**
Encompasses quadratic surds in the denominator, Euler-style linear substitutions under radicals, and symmetric algebraic forms such as expressions with reciprocal quadratic terms.

5. **Partial Fractions & Rational Functions (7 questions, 2019-2026; 9 total, last asked 2026)**
Deals with decomposing rational functions into basic denominators. Questions often require completing the square or separating linear factors before applying standard logarithmic or inverse tangent antiderivatives.

## What the questions look like

Antiderivative questions set by the NTA regularly test specific structural patterns:

- **Factoring Out Leading Powers**: The integrand contains a polynomial inside a fractional power in the denominator. Factoring out the dominant power produces an expression whose derivative matches the numerator up to a constant scalar.
- **Exponential with Function and Derivative**: An integrand of the form e^x multiplied by an algebraic expression decomposes into a function plus its exact derivative, allowing the integral to collapse into e^x multiplied by that function.
- **Trigonometric Reduction to Tangent and Secant**: Denominators feature quadratic or quartic combinations of sine and cosine. Dividing the numerator and denominator by cosine squared transforms the integral into an algebraic substitution in terms of tangent.
- **Function Defined by Antiderivative with Initial Value**: The problem defines f(x) as an antiderivative satisfying a given value at an initial argument, then asks for the value of f at another point.

## How to practise it

Solving indefinite integrals reliably requires proactive pattern recognition:

- **Look for the derivative in the numerator**: Before embarking on elaborate transformations, inspect whether the numerator can be rewritten as the derivative of any portion of the denominator, either directly or after dividing numerator and denominator by an appropriate power of x.
- **Split exponential integrands**: When e^x appears multiplied by a rational or radical bracket, check whether the bracket splits into two terms where the second is the derivative of the first. If so, avoid integration by parts and apply the standard exponential identity directly.
- **Substitute auxiliary trigonometric variables**: For expressions containing differences of squares under square roots, consider standard trigonometric substitutions using sine, secant, or tangent to eliminate radicals cleanly.
- **Determine the integration constant promptly**: When an initial functional condition is specified, solve for the constant of integration immediately upon completing the antiderivative to avoid forgetting it during subsequent numerical evaluations.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I differentiate the options in multiple-choice questions?
Differentiating four complicated answer choices can be time-consuming and error-prone, especially when options involve intricate logarithms and square roots. While differentiation can serve as an emergency fallback, spotting the intended substitution usually yields the antiderivative in fewer lines of work. Furthermore, this shortcut is useless in numerical answer questions.

### What is the most common algebraic trick in recent papers?
The most recurring technique in recent NTA papers is factoring x to the nth power out of a radical denominator. This creates negative powers of x in both the numerator and the remaining radicand, setting up a clean substitution where the radicand becomes the new variable.

### How does mastery of indefinite integrals affect definite integration?
Many definite integrals in JEE Main can be resolved directly using symmetry or properties like King's rule without finding antiderivatives. However, questions involving piecewise functions, absolute values, or non-symmetric domains still require exact antiderivatives, making proficiency in this chapter essential.
