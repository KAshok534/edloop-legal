## How often it comes up

Quadratic Equations is a reliable chapter in JEE Main Mathematics. In the papers we hold from 2019 to 2026, it gave **185 questions in 162 papers**, about **1.1 questions per paper**, and it appeared in 147 of those papers. By share of Mathematics questions (4.0%) it ranks **12th of 29 Mathematics chapters** in that period.

The share has held steady across two decades. From 2002 to 2018 it was also 4.0% of Mathematics, with 1.4 questions per paper; in the last three exam years (2024 to 2026) it is 4.4%, with 68 questions across the 58 papers we hold. The busiest year was 2021, with 34 questions across 26 papers. Since 2019 there has been no year without it, appearing in 147 of the 162 papers we hold.

It is a Class 11 chapter, part of NTA's Unit 2: Complex Numbers and Quadratic Equations.

## Most-asked topics

Three topics split the chapter. From 2019 to 2026:

- **Roots and Coefficients**: 82 questions. The classic topic: sum and product of roots, formation of new equations from given ones.
- **Higher Degree and Special Equations**: 81 questions. Equations involving absolute values, surds, logarithms and exponentials.
- **Quadratic Expressions and Location of Roots**: 22 questions. Finding conditions on parameters so that roots lie in a given interval.

All three topics appeared in 2026.

## What the questions look like

Roots and coefficients questions give a quadratic with one or two unknown parameters and ask for a sum or product of related quantities, or for the range of a parameter that keeps the roots real and distinct. The standard identity relating roots to coefficients is the entry point in almost all of them.

Higher degree and special equations cover a wide range: an equation with an absolute value that splits into two cases, a logarithmic or exponential equation that reduces to a quadratic after substitution, or a polynomial of degree four that factors over the rationals. These questions reward the habit of checking for extraneous solutions.

Location of roots questions fix an integer or interval and ask what values of a parameter keep both roots in a specified range. The method is to translate each condition into an inequality on the discriminant, the value at the boundary points, and the vertex position.

Of the 185 questions since 2019, 52 were numerical-answer questions, so the final number has to be exact.

## How to practise it

Begin with roots and coefficients: practise forming equations given symmetric expressions of the roots and deriving new equations whose roots are functions of the original ones. These questions are structured and reward speed once the identities are fluent.

Then work through higher degree and special equations as a separate set. For absolute-value equations, always split at the critical point and check each case. For logarithmic or exponential forms, write the substitution at the top of the working so the structure is visible.

Leave location of roots for last, since it needs both the sign conditions and the discriminant condition at once; work through several parameter-range examples before moving to timed practice.
