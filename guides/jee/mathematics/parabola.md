## What the questions look like

Examiners repeatedly employ distinct geometric patterns when designing parabola problems:

- **Focal Chord Parametric Relations**: A chord passes through the focus (a, 0), establishing that the parameters of its endpoints satisfy t1 multiplied by t2 equals minus one. Questions evaluate harmonic mean properties of focal lengths or coordinate ratios.
- **Right Angle Subtended at the Vertex**: A chord connecting two points on the curve subtends a right angle at the vertex, establishing that the product of the parameter values equals minus four.
- **Locus of Triangle Centroids and Midpoints**: Inscribed triangles with one vertex at the origin and remaining vertices moving along the curve under perpendicularity or area constraints, asking for the algebraic curve traced by the centroid.
- **Chord Bisected at a Given Point**: Finding the equation of a secant chord whose midpoint is specified, solved using the classical T equals S1 relation.

## How to practise it

Solving parabola problems with confidence relies on parametric coordinates:

- **Adopt parametric coordinates immediately**: Instead of working with Cartesian coordinates (x, y), label points on y squared equals 4ax as (a t squared, 2at). This replaces two-variable systems with single-parameter algebraic expressions.
- **Memorise focal chord parameter properties**: Remember that if a chord passes through the focus, the endpoint parameters satisfy t1 t2 equals minus one. Consequently, the length of the focal chord equals a multiplied by the square of t minus one over t plus four.
- **Use the chord with given midpoint identity**: To write the line containing a chord whose midpoint is (x1, y1), apply T equals S1 directly. For y squared equals 4ax, this simplifies to y y1 minus 2a(x plus x1) equals y1 squared minus 4a x1.
- **Exploit vertex subtended angle identities**: If a chord joining t1 and t2 subtends a right angle at the origin, the slope product condition reduces to t1 t2 equals minus four. This immediately fixes the x-intercept of the chord line at 4a on the axis of symmetry.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happened to the normal line formulas in the recent syllabus?
While general tangent and normal theory for arbitrary calculus functions was removed, standard coordinate geometry of conics still regularly features problems involving perpendicular chords, focal chords, and geometric intersections. Using parametric representations remains the most reliable problem-solving method.

### Why is the semi-latus rectum the harmonic mean of focal segments?
If a focal chord has endpoints with focal distances SP and SQ, the reciprocal sum one over SP plus one over SQ equals one over a, which is a constant independent of the chord inclination. Thus, the semi-latus rectum 2a is the harmonic mean of the two focal segments SP and SQ.

### How do I identify the axis and directrix of a shifted parabola?
Complete the square for the quadratic variable to bring the equation into the form (Y minus k) squared equals 4a(X minus h). The axis of symmetry is Y equals k, the tangent at the vertex is X equals h, the focus is at (h plus a, k), and the directrix is the line X equals h minus a.
