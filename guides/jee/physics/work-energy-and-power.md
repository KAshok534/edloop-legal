## How often it comes up

Work, Energy and Power is a mid-sized chapter in JEE Main Physics. In the papers we hold from 2019 to 2026, it gave **151 questions in 162 papers**, about **0.9 questions per paper**, appearing in 122 of those papers. By share of Physics questions (3.3%) it ranks **14th of 29 Physics chapters** in that period.

The chapter came up less often in the AIEEE years (3.7% from 2002 to 2018), and the recent trend continues at a similar level. In the last three exam years (2024 to 2026) it is 2.9%, with 45 questions in 58 papers. Since 2019 there has been no year without it.

It is a Class 11 chapter covered under NTA's Unit 4: Work, Energy and Power. Its ideas feed directly into Rotational Motion, Oscillations and Thermodynamics, so a clear understanding here pays off across many chapters.

## Most-asked topics

Four topics split the questions. From 2019 to 2026:

- **Energy and Conservation**: 69 questions. The largest share by far, covering kinetic and potential energy, the work-energy theorem and energy conservation in mechanical systems.
- **Collisions**: 37 questions. Elastic and inelastic collisions, coefficient of restitution, and finding velocities after impact.
- **Work**: 27 questions. Work done by constant and variable forces, work done against friction and by conservative forces.
- **Power**: 18 questions. Instantaneous and average power, often from a force-velocity or force-time relationship.

All four topics appeared again in 2026.

## What the questions look like

Energy and conservation questions cover a wide range. Some give a potential energy versus position graph and ask you to rank the force in different segments; the slope of the graph is the magnitude of the force. Others set up a particle moving under a known velocity-position relationship and ask for the work done over a given interval. A classic variant asks which of two paths from the same height a bead arrives at the bottom first.

Collision questions test whether a collision is elastic or perfectly inelastic, then apply conservation of momentum and, where appropriate, conservation of kinetic energy. A common recent variant converts lost kinetic energy into heat and asks for the temperature rise of the combined body.

Work questions use the dot product of force and displacement, sometimes over a curved path where only the component along the displacement matters, and sometimes with a force that varies with position.

Power questions typically give a time-varying force or a velocity-position relationship and ask for instantaneous power at a specific moment.

Of the 151 questions from 2019 to 2026, 48 were numerical-answer type, which is a high fraction. Exact answers matter in this chapter.

## How to practise it

Start with energy conservation, because it appears in almost half the questions in this chapter and it also appears in Rotational Motion and Oscillations. Practice identifying when only conservative forces act (so mechanical energy is constant) and when friction or drag does negative work (so you need the work-energy theorem instead).

After that, do collisions. Keep the two cases separate in your mind: elastic collisions conserve both momentum and kinetic energy, perfectly inelastic ones only conserve momentum. Always check whether the problem specifies the coefficient of restitution; if it does, that is the second equation you need.

Work and power are smaller topics. For work, the key skill is computing the dot product when the force varies; a short set of practice problems is usually enough. For power, the formula P = F · v makes most questions short once you have the velocity.

Since 48 of the 151 questions from 2019 to 2026 were numerical-answer type, practise keeping intermediate results exact and checking units at the end.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which topic in this chapter has the most questions?

Energy and conservation, with 69 questions from 2019 to 2026, nearly half the chapter. Collisions follow with 37, then work with 27 and power with 18.

### Are Work, Energy and Power questions often numerical-answer?

Yes, more than in many other chapters. Of the 151 questions from 2019 to 2026, 48 were numerical-answer type. This means exact calculation is needed; elimination strategies alone will not carry you here.

### How does this chapter connect to Rotational Motion?

Energy conservation is used in both chapters. Rolling questions in Rotational Motion split kinetic energy into translational and rotational parts, so the methods you practise here carry over directly. See [Rotational Motion](/jee/pyq/physics/rotational-motion) for how that works.
