## Most-asked topics

Historical questions fall into two primary subject divisions:

- **Modulation**: 56 questions since 2011, with 44 asked between 2019 and 2026, last tested in 2023. This area examines amplitude modulation principles, modulation index mu from peak-to-peak signal limits, sideband frequencies, carrier power, total radiated power, and transmission bandwidth.
- **Communication Basics**: 48 total questions, including 38 from 2019 to 2026, last tested in 2023. Core concepts assess line-of-sight radio horizon distances for elevated antennas, space wave propagation, sky wave ionospheric reflection, tropospheric ground waves, terminology matching, and frequency band allocations for television, FM, and satellite services.

## What the questions look like

Past questions from this chapter featured straightforward numerical evaluations and conceptual matching:

- **Modulation index from waveform envelopes**: Finding the modulation index from maximum and minimum peak-to-peak voltages of an amplitude-modulated carrier wave.
- **Line-of-sight horizon distance**: Calculating the maximum line-of-sight communication range between two elevated antennas of specified heights above the Earth.
- **Transmission bandwidth**: Determining the total bandwidth required for transmitting an audio message on a high-frequency carrier wave.
- **Matching communication terminology**: Matching terms like attenuation, transducer, repeater, and demodulation with their definitions.

## How to practise it

Because communication systems is excluded from the current syllabus, students preparing exclusively for JEE Main should not allocate active preparation time to this chapter.

If revising past year papers for general practice or state engineering entrances that retain the topic, focus on the modulation index formula A max minus A min divided by A max plus A min, alongside the line-of-sight distance formula square root of two R h.

Learn basic definitions of attenuation, amplification, modulation, and demodulation. Familiarize yourself with standard broadcast frequency allocations for radio and television.
