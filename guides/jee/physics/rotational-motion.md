## How often it comes up

Rotational Motion is one of the steadiest chapters in JEE Main Physics. Across the papers we hold from 2019 to 2026, it gave **241 questions in 162 papers**, about **1.5 questions per paper**, and it appeared in 158 of those 162 papers. That makes it the **3rd largest of 29 Physics chapters** in that period, with 5.3% of all Physics questions.

The share has not moved much over two decades. In the AIEEE and early JEE Main years (2002 to 2018) it was 5.5% of Physics, and in the last three exam years (2024 to 2026) it is again 5.5%: 85 questions in 58 papers. Since 2019 there has been no year without it, and it was missing from only a handful of the 162 papers. If you are planning JEE Main 2027, treat it as a chapter you cannot leave out.

It is a Class 11 chapter (NTA's Unit 5: Rotational Motion), but it leans on Laws of Motion and Work, Energy and Power, so it rewards students who are already comfortable with forces and energy.

## Most-asked topics

From 2019 to 2026, the questions split across five topics:

- **Moment of Inertia**: 71 questions. The single most-asked topic in the chapter.
- **Torque & Angular Momentum**: 63 questions. Over the whole archive (89 questions) it is just ahead of moment of inertia.
- **Rolling Motion**: 44 questions.
- **Rotational Dynamics**: 35 questions.
- **Centre of Mass**: 28 questions.

All five topics were asked in 2026, so none of them is safe to skip.

## What the questions look like

Most moment of inertia questions are built from shapes you already know: rods joined into a T or a square frame, discs and rings, spheres touching each other. The work is in choosing the axis and using the parallel or perpendicular axis theorem cleanly, not in long algebra.

Torque and angular momentum questions often hide the rotation. A classic version asks for the angular momentum of one moving car about another on a parallel track, or of masses stuck to a free bar. If you remember that angular momentum about a point is defined for straight-line motion too, these become short.

Rolling questions usually combine energy and rotation: a body rolls up a curve or down an incline and you must find how far it gets or which body it is. Rotational dynamics questions put a pulley with mass, or a rod that suddenly loses a support, into a Newton's-laws problem.

A large part of this chapter now comes as numerical-answer questions: 102 of the 241 questions since 2019 had no options at all, so you need the exact value, not an elimination trick.

## How to practise it

Start with moment of inertia, because the other topics reuse it. Learn the standard results for a rod, ring, disc, cylinder and sphere, then practise only the axis theorems until combined shapes feel routine.

Next, do torque and angular momentum together with conservation of angular momentum, since many questions switch between the two. Leave rolling for after you are confident with energy conservation, then mix all topics in timed sets. Because so many questions are numerical, check units and powers of ten before you enter an answer.
