## What the questions look like

Stress, strain and moduli questions are mostly about wires and rods. A typical question gives two wires of different materials, lengths or cross-sections loaded with the same force (or equal extension) and asks for the ratio of some property. Another common type gives a stress-strain graph for several materials and asks which has the largest Young's modulus (the steepest slope). Bulk modulus questions involve a liquid or solid under pressure and ask for the percentage change in volume. Rigidity modulus questions ask for the displacement of one face of a block under a shear force.

Elastic energy questions calculate the energy stored in a stretched spring or wire, or the spring constant for two springs connected in series or parallel. A popular variant involves a metal wire pulled taut at a given temperature, then cooled: it develops tension because thermal contraction is opposed, and the question asks for the tension or the temperature at which it reaches a specified value.

A notable feature of this chapter: 42 of the 99 questions from 2019 to 2026 were numerical-answer type, a very high fraction. Exact computation is needed here.

## How to practise it

Start with Young's modulus, because it appears in roughly three-quarters of the questions in this chapter. Work through the basic formula (stress equals Young's modulus times strain) until you can rearrange it for any variable. Then practise with two or three wires: same load, different areas; same length, different materials; same cross-section, different forces.

After that, do bulk modulus with liquids and gases, then rigidity modulus with blocks. Keep the three moduli clearly separate in your mind because questions sometimes test whether you are applying the right one.

For elastic energy, the formula (energy is half the product of stress and strain and volume, or equivalently half force times extension) is enough for most questions. Thermal stress questions need both the formula for thermal expansion and Hooke's law at the same time, so practise those together.

Because 42 of the 99 questions from 2019 to 2026 were numerical-answer type, check your arithmetic carefully and keep track of powers of ten.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which modulus is asked the most in this chapter?

Young's modulus, by a wide margin. Most stress, strain and moduli questions from 2019 to 2026 involve wires or rods under tension, where Young's modulus is the relevant quantity.

### Why is the fraction of numerical-answer questions so high in Elasticity?

The quantities in this chapter (stress, strain, energy, extension) tend to have clean numerical values once you plug in the given dimensions and forces. The checker can assign an exact answer, so NTA makes many of these questions numerical-answer type. Plan to get the number right, not just the method.

### What is thermal stress and when does it appear?

Thermal stress appears when a rod or wire is clamped at both ends and then heated or cooled. Because it cannot expand or contract freely, it develops a stress equal to Young's modulus times the coefficient of linear expansion times the temperature change. Expect at least one such question from time to time, usually asking for the stress or the temperature at which the stress reaches a given value.
