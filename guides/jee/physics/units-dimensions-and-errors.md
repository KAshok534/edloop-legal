## How often it comes up

Units, Dimensions and Errors is one of the most reliable chapters in JEE Main Physics. In the papers we hold from 2019 to 2026, it contributed **235 questions across 162 papers**, about **1.5 questions per paper**, appearing in 156 of those papers. That places it **4th of 29 Physics chapters** by share of questions in that period, with 5.1% of all Physics questions.

The chapter has grown noticeably in recent years. From 2002 to 2018 it was 3.5% of Physics; in the last three exam years (2024 to 2026) it rose to 6.5%, with 101 questions in 58 papers. Since 2019 there has been no year without it, and it appeared in 156 of the 162 papers we hold. If you are preparing for JEE Main 2027, this is a chapter you cannot leave out.

It is a Class 11 chapter covered under NTA's Unit 1: Units and Measurements.

## Most-asked topics

From 2019 to 2026, four topics account for all the questions:

- **Dimensional Analysis**: 118 questions. By far the largest share, covering deriving formulas, checking consistency and matching physical quantities with their dimensions.
- **Errors and Significant Figures**: 65 questions. Absolute and relative errors, propagation rules, and rounding to the right number of significant figures.
- **Measuring Instruments**: 43 questions. Vernier calipers and screw gauges, least count, zero error, and corrected readings.
- **Units and SI System**: 9 questions. Definitions of base units, conversions, and non-SI units still in use.

All four topics appeared again in 2026.

## What the questions look like

Dimensional analysis questions usually give you a formula or equation with unknown constants and ask for the dimensions of each. A common variant gives a modified physics equation (such as a pressure or velocity formula) where one constant replaces a quantity, and you must find what dimensions make both sides consistent. Some questions ask you to match a list of physical quantities with their dimensional formulas.

Error questions typically involve an experiment where several quantities are measured, each with its own percentage error. You apply the propagation rule (errors add for products and quotients, with powers multiplying) and find the maximum error in the final result. Significant figures questions ask you to add or multiply measured values and state the answer with the correct number of figures.

Measuring instrument questions almost always set up a screw gauge or vernier caliper with a given least count and a zero error, then ask for the corrected reading of an object.

Of the 235 questions from 2019 to 2026, only 25 were numerical-answer type, so this chapter leans heavily towards multiple-choice.

## How to practise it

Start with dimensional analysis because it appears in roughly half the questions in this chapter. Work through the standard quantities (velocity, acceleration, force, energy, pressure, charge, magnetic field) until their dimensions feel automatic. Then practise the formula-derivation type, where you express an unknown constant in terms of known quantities and read off its dimensions.

Move to errors next. Learn the propagation rules once and apply them to a fresh set of experiments each day: density from mass and volume, resistance from voltage and current, viscosity from terminal velocity. Keep significant figures as a short, final pass since the rules are simple but easy to confuse under time pressure.

For measuring instruments, draw a diagram of the vernier or screw gauge, label the least count and zero error, and practise corrected readings with both positive and negative zero errors.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which topic in this chapter carries the most questions?

Dimensional analysis, with 118 questions from 2019 to 2026. It is more than half the chapter on its own, so if time is short, start there.

### Are error calculations asked as numerical-answer questions?

Occasionally. Of the 235 questions from 2019 to 2026, only 25 were numerical-answer type. Most questions in this chapter are multiple-choice, so the main challenge is choosing the right formula and not making a sign or power error.

### What instruments does JEE Main test in this chapter?

The vernier caliper and the screw gauge are the two most common, with 43 measuring-instrument questions from 2019 to 2026. Questions focus on reading the scale, applying the least count, and correcting for zero error, not on derivations of the instruments themselves.
