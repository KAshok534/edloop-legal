## What the questions look like

Oscillating system problems typically require deriving the effective restoring force. A classic question cuts a uniform coil spring of stiffness 15 N/m into two pieces in a 1 : 3 length ratio and asks for the spring constant of the shorter segment. Because spring stiffness is inversely proportional to unstretched length, shorter segments become stiffer. Other questions evaluate the oscillation period of a cylinder floating upright in a liquid when gently depressed and released, requiring students to equate net restoring buoyant force to mass times acceleration.

Kinematic questions test the harmonic equations x = A sin(ωt + φ). Students compute the earliest time when velocity reaches zero (at extreme displacement) or when acceleration vanishes (passing through the mean position). Phase difference problems determine whether two oscillators moving along parallel lines pass each other heading in identical or opposite directions.

Energy questions center on the quadratic dependence of energy on position. You will encounter questions noting that kinetic energy oscillates at an angular frequency of 176 rad/s, asking for the natural frequency of the physical oscillator itself. Because kinetic energy completes two full cycles for every single mechanical cycle of the mass, the oscillator frequency is half the kinetic energy frequency.

Numerical entry problems represent a noticeable portion: 38 of the 132 questions between 2019 and 2026 were numerical answer items.

## How to practise it

Master the defining differential equation of simple harmonic motion: acceleration equals negative ω² times displacement. Whenever you encounter an unfamiliar physical setup, displace the system by a small distance x from its equilibrium position, write the net restoring force, and show that force is proportional to negative x. The ratio of the restoring constant to system inertia immediately gives ω².

Remember the energy frequency relation: while displacement, velocity, and acceleration complete one cycle in time period T, kinetic energy and potential energy complete two full cycles in the same duration. The frequency of energy oscillation is exactly twice the frequency of mechanical motion.

For spring combinations, treat springs like capacitors in electrical circuits: springs in parallel add their stiffnesses directly (k1 + k2), while springs in series combine reciprocally. For simple pendulums, verify that time period scales with the square root of length divided by effective acceleration, remembering to modify effective g inside accelerating elevators or vehicles.

### Why does a shorter spring have a higher spring constant?

A spring's stiffness is inversely proportional to its active coil length. When a spring is cut into smaller segments, each unit length must stretch by the same absolute amount to produce a given overall tension, meaning the shorter segment requires much more force to achieve a unit total elongation.
