# JEE Main Practical Chemistry & Qualitative Analysis: PYQ Trends & Preparation Guide

Practical Chemistry & Qualitative Analysis covers laboratory techniques, systematic inorganic cation and anion detection (salt analysis), organic functional group tests, titrimetric methods, and double salt preparations. In recent testing sessions, this unit has gained significant momentum in JEE Main.

## How often it comes up

During 2019 to 2026, Practical Chemistry & Qualitative Analysis registered 97 questions across 162 exam papers, accounting for 2.1% of chemistry questions and holding rank 26 of 31 chapters. In the earlier testing span from 2002 to 2018, it contributed 16 questions across 38 papers, delivering a 1.2% share. Across the recent 2024 to 2026 cycle, question counts rose sharply to 51 questions across 58 papers, capturing a 3.3% share. The testing weight has trended up across recent shifts.

The chapter has appeared every year from 2019 to 2026. Students met an average of 0.6 questions per paper over 2019 to 2026, rising to 0.9 questions per paper in the 2024 to 2026 period. Eleven numerical answer questions were included between 2019 and 2026.

## Most-asked topics

The syllabus divides across four distinct areas:

- Salt Analysis: Forms the core of the chapter with 80 total questions (66 in 2019 to 2026), examined continuously through 2026. Key areas test group reagent precipitations (groups zero through six), common ion effect in selective sulfide or hydroxide precipitation, confirmatory tests for cations (such as lead, copper, iron, aluminum, barium, and calcium), and anion identification tests (chromyl chloride test for chloride, brown ring test for nitrate, and Griess-Ilosvay reaction for nitrite).
- Organic Practicals: Yielded 15 total questions (all 15 during 2019 to 2026), evaluated through 2026. Focuses on functional group detection: Lucas test for alcohol classification, 2,4-DNP, Tollens and Fehling tests for carbonyls, carbylamine test for primary amines, and sodium bicarbonate effervescence for carboxylic acids.
- Titrimetric Analysis: Contributed 15 total questions (13 in 2019 to 2026), tested through 2026. Covers acid-base indicators, requirements of primary standards, and redox titrations of Mohr's salt or oxalic acid against potassium permanganate and potassium dichromate.
- Inorganic Preparations: Recorded 3 total questions (all 3 in 2019 to 2026), last assessed in 2024. Concepts explore synthesis of Mohr's salt (ferrous ammonium sulfate) and potash alum, including the preventive role of dilute sulfuric acid against hydrolysis.

## What the questions look like

Examiners test exact laboratory observations and chemical equations. Salt analysis questions frequently provide experimental sequences: adding specific group reagents, observing precipitate colors, and adding excess reagents to form soluble complex ions.

In organic functional testing, questions often present four isomeric compounds and ask which single test reagent can differentiate them. Titration questions evaluate indicator color changes and criteria that distinguish primary standards like oxalic acid from secondary standards like sodium hydroxide.

## How to practise it

Because qualitative analysis tests exact laboratory details, structure revision around tables:

1. Memorize group separation reagents: Know the analytical group, added reagent, and precipitating form for cations from lead and silver up to magnesium.
2. Learn classic confirmatory colored complexes: Memorize formulas for Turnbull's blue, Prussian blue, brown ring complex, Nessler's precipitate, and nickel dimethylglyoxime.
3. Understand solubility mechanisms: Explain why lead chloride dissolves in hot water while silver chloride dissolves in aqueous ammonia.
4. Review standard organic tests: Maintain a quick reference chart for Lucas reagent, neutral ferric chloride, bromine water, and iodoform reactions.
5. Review titration stoichiometry: Practise n-factor calculations for permanganate and dichromate oxidations.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why has practical chemistry grown so much in recent JEE Main papers?
Following curriculum pruning in other theoretical inorganic topics, the National Testing Agency increased direct practical questions to test genuine laboratory understanding.

### What is the purpose of adding dilute sulfuric acid during Mohr's salt preparation?
Dilute sulfuric acid prevents the premature hydrolysis of ferrous sulfate into insoluble basic iron salts during crystallization.

### How do I prepare for anion confirmatory reactions?
Focus on the wet tests for halides (chromyl chloride and silver nitrate), sulfate (barium chloride), and nitrate (brown ring test with freshly prepared ferrous sulfate). Step-by-step solutions for each one are in the EdLoop app.
