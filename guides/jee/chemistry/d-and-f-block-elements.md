## How to practise it

Thorough preparation requires mastering electron bookkeeping alongside industrial chemical equations:

1. Memorize 3d electron configurations: Be completely fluent writing ground state and ionic configurations for scandium through zinc, noting anomalies at chromium and copper.
2. Master spin-only magnetic calculations: Be quick at deriving unpaired electrons (n) from oxidation states and calculating the resulting spin moment in Bohr magnetons.
3. Balance permanganate and dichromate half-reactions: Know electron transfers for MnO₄⁻ yielding Mn²⁺ in acid, MnO₂ in neutral solution, and MnO₄²⁻ in strong base.
4. Understand consequences of lanthanoid contraction: Remember that poor shielding by 4f electrons causes almost identical atomic radii for Zr/Hf and Nb/Ta.
5. Review chromyl chloride test steps: Understand the formation of red vapor chromyl chloride and its conversion to yellow lead chromate precipitate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do zirconium and hafnium have nearly identical atomic radii?
Due to lanthanoid contraction, the filling of 4f orbitals prior to 5d elements provides inadequate shielding against increased nuclear charge, canceling the normal size expansion expected between successive rows.

### Which oxidation states of lanthanoids are exceptionally stable?
While +3 is the universal stable state, Ce⁴⁺ achieves stability with an empty f shell, while Eu²⁺ achieves stability through a half-filled 4f subshell.
