# JEE Main Biomolecules: PYQ Trends & Preparation Guide

Biomolecules bridges organic chemistry with biological structures. The chapter covers carbohydrates (glucose open-chain and cyclic structures, mutarotation, reducing versus non-reducing disaccharides, polysaccharides), amino acids (zwitterions, isoelectric points, essential versus non-essential types), proteins (peptide linkages, primary through quaternary structures, denaturation), nucleic acids (DNA and RNA nucleotides, phosphodiester bonds), and vitamins.

## Most-asked topics

The syllabus organizes across three core topics:

- Carbohydrates: The leading topic with 91 total questions (73 in 2019 to 2026), evaluated continuously through 2026. Key areas investigate glucose chemical reactions (forming pentaacetate with acetic anhydride, saccharic acid with nitric acid, and n-hexane with HI and red phosphorus), cyclic alpha/beta anomers, mutarotation, Haworth projections, glycosidic linkages in maltose, lactose, and sucrose, and the structural basis of invert sugar.
- Amino Acids & Proteins: Contributed 73 total questions (60 in 2019 to 2026), tested through 2026. Focuses on classification into essential and non-essential amino acids, acidic/basic/neutral side chains, zwitterionic forms, peptide bond formation, secondary protein stabilization via hydrogen bonding (alpha-helix and beta-pleated sheets), and protein denaturation disrupting secondary and tertiary conformations while preserving primary structure.
- Vitamins, Nucleic Acids & Hormones: Produced 43 total questions (29 in 2019 to 2026), active through 2026. Examines water-soluble versus fat-soluble vitamins (deficiency diseases like scurvy, rickets, beriberi, and night blindness), chemical components of DNA versus RNA (purine and pyrimidine base pairing), chirality of the pentose sugar backbone, and thyroid/pancreatic hormones.

## What the questions look like

Examiners test structural knowledge directly through diagrams and reaction matching. A recurring carbohydrate question presents four disaccharide structures and asks candidates to identify the non-reducing sugar by checking whether all anomeric carbons participate in glycosidic linkages (as in sucrose).

Protein questions often evaluate amino acid structures, asking students to identify essential amino acids, count peptide linkages in a drawn oligopeptide, or explain why glycine is the only standard proteinogenic amino acid lacking an asymmetric chiral alpha-carbon.

## How to practise it

Prepare for biomolecules by memorizing exact chemical formulas, linkages, and test responses:

1. Memorize glucose chemical reactions: Glucose with bromine water gives gluconic acid (monocarboxylic); with concentrated nitric acid gives saccharic acid (dicarboxylic); with acetic anhydride gives pentaacetate confirming five hydroxyl groups.
2. Note why sucrose is a non-reducing sugar: In sucrose, the hemiacetal hydroxyl of alpha-D-glucose and the hemiketal hydroxyl of beta-D-fructose link together through a glycosidic bond, leaving no free anomeric carbon to reduce Tollens or Fehling solutions.
3. Classify vitamins by solubility: Vitamins B and C are water-soluble (excreted readily, requiring regular intake, except vitamin B12); vitamins A, D, E, and K are fat-soluble (stored in liver and adipose tissue).
4. Identify essential amino acids: Memorize the ten essential amino acids (valine, leucine, isoleucine, lysine, methionine, threonine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, histidine, arginine).
5. Understand nucleic acid structures: Adenine pairs with thymine via two hydrogen bonds; guanine pairs with cytosine via three hydrogen bonds. In RNA, uracil replaces thymine.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is glycine optically inactive?
Glycine possesses two hydrogen atoms bonded to its alpha carbon, giving it an internal plane of symmetry and preventing it from having a chiral center, unlike all other natural amino acids.

### What is invert sugar?
Sucrose is dextrorotatory, but upon acid hydrolysis it yields an equimolar mixture of dextrorotatory D-glucose and more strongly levorotatory D-fructose, resulting in an overall levorotatory mixture called invert sugar.

### What remains intact when a protein undergoes denaturation?
Denaturation by heat or pH changes disrupts hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions, destroying secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structures, but leaves the covalent primary peptide backbone completely intact. Step-by-step solutions for each one are in the EdLoop app.
