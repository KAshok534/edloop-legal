## What the questions look like

JEE Main problems in chemical thermodynamics combine thermodynamic sign conventions with multi-step arithmetic:

- **Hess's law algebraic summation**: Given combustion or formation enthalpies for intermediate reactions, students manipulate and sum algebraic equations to evaluate the unknown standard enthalpy of formation for a target compound.
- **Isothermal work and heat calculations**: Evaluating expansion work, change in internal energy, and absorbed heat for an ideal gas expanding reversibly or irreversibly against external pressure.
- **Standard Gibbs energy and equilibrium constants**: Connecting measured equilibrium constants or reaction quotients at specified temperatures with standard enthalpy and entropy changes.
- **Bond energy bookkeeping**: Calculating individual average bond enthalpies, such as carbon-hydrogen bond strength, from the enthalpy of formation of methane combined with graphite atomization and hydrogen gas dissociation enthalpies.

## How to practise it

Pay rigorous attention to IUPAC sign conventions used in chemistry. Work done on the system by surroundings during compression is positive, while work done by the system on surroundings during expansion is negative: w equals minus P external multiplied by delta V. Heat absorbed by the system is positive, and heat released is negative.

For thermochemical cycles using bond energies, remember that reaction enthalpy equals the sum of bond enthalpies of broken bonds in reactants minus the sum of bond enthalpies of formed bonds in products. Contrast this with standard enthalpies of formation, where reaction enthalpy equals products minus reactants.

When calculating spontaneity using Gibbs energy, convert enthalpy and entropy values to matching units before evaluating delta H minus T delta S. Enthalpies are typically given in kilojoules while entropies are given in joules per Kelvin, which is a frequent source of calculation errors.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are there so many numerical answer questions in thermodynamics?
Thermodynamics questions evaluate definite quantities like enthalpies, entropies, and equilibrium constants, making them ideal for Section B numerical questions where 97 problems appeared between 2019 and 2026.

### How do chemical thermodynamics sign conventions differ from physics?
In chemistry, the first law is written as delta U equals q plus w, where work done on the system is positive. In physics, it is commonly written as delta Q equals delta U plus delta W, where work done by the system is positive.

### How do I determine whether a reaction is spontaneous at all temperatures?
Examine the signs of delta H and delta S. When delta H is negative and delta S is positive, delta G is negative at all temperatures, ensuring spontaneity regardless of thermal energy. Step-by-step solutions for each one are in the EdLoop app.
